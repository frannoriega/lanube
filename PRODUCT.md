# Product

## Register

product

## Users

**Community members** (primary): students, researchers, freelancers, and small teams who reserve coworking desks, lab space, auditorium, and meeting rooms at La Nube. They interact with the system to book, check in, and track their own usage. Context: recurring use, modest tech literacy, Spanish-speaking (LatAm). They discover events on the public landing and sign up through the form pages.

**Staff / Admins** (secondary): La Nube staff who approve or reject reservations, manage check-in/out, track incidents, and oversee the space. Higher-frequency, task-dense sessions. They need efficiency and clear status signals, not ceremony.

## Product Purpose

La Nube is a coworking space management system for a Polo Tecnológico — a community technology hub. It handles resource reservations, event registration, and space administration. Success means any community member can reserve a space in under two minutes, and any admin can process the day's queue without hunting for information.

The public surface (landing + events) is the first impression for prospective members. The app surface is where the recurring relationship lives.

## Brand Personality

Warm · local · collaborative. La Nube feels like a real place run by people who care about their community, not a product shipped by a vendor. Confident without being corporate. Functional without being utilitarian.

## Anti-references

- **Corporate coworking chains** (WeWork, Regus): stock photography, enterprise coldness, brand-as-status. La Nube is community-owned, not investor-owned.
- **University admin portals**: circa-2015 utility UIs — tables everywhere, no hierarchy, no warmth, forms that feel punitive.

## Design Principles

1. **Community first, system second.** The interface should feel like it belongs to the people using it. No vendor coldness, no enterprise abstraction.
2. **Local identity over interchangeability.** La Nube's particular character — the place, the community, the blue-and-cyan palette — should be visible at a glance. Not swappable with any other tool.
3. **Approachable authority.** Admin surfaces need clarity and efficiency, but never institutional coldness. Admins are community members too.
4. **Progressive disclosure.** Members see simplicity; admins see depth. Surface complexity only when the user is ready for it.
5. **Warmth through system-level choices.** Brand warmth comes from coherent, deliberate design decisions — spacing, voice, color consistency — not decorative add-ons.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA. 4.5:1 contrast for body text, keyboard navigation throughout, ARIA labels on interactive elements. Interface is fully in Spanish (LatAm).
